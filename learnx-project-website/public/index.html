01 / Research domain

From separate tools
to a shared approach.

LearnX investigates how multimodal learning tools can share curriculum grounding, orchestration and validation while retaining specialized component pipelines.

Literature survey

Specialized AI methods provide the building blocks.

The project literature explores parameter-efficient model adaptation, reflective generation, promptable segmentation, retrieval-augmented generation and multilingual speech recognition.

Model adaptation

LoRA and QLoRA support adapting language models with fewer trainable parameters. LearnX applies adaptation to structured visual prompts and assessment generation.

Research paper, references [1–2]

Visual grounding

SAM 2 supports point and box segmentation, while vision-language models help inspect generated educational images and selected regions.

Research paper, references [3, 8]

Reflection and retrieval

Reflexion and Self-Refine inform critique-and-retry workflows. Retrieval-augmented generation provides curriculum evidence for generated answers and questions.

Research paper, references [5–6, 16]

Multilingual learning

Whisper and hybrid retrieval provide a foundation for spoken revision, alongside language-specific speech and handwriting pipelines.

Research gap

Learning context is difficult to carry between modalities.

Learners often use unrelated applications for diagrams, quizzes, spoken explanations and handwritten notes. A shared approach to context and evidence remains a research challenge.

  • Fragmented learning activities: visual, assessment, audio and note workflows commonly operate separately.
  • Reliability of generated content: useful outputs still require grounding, factual checks and evaluation.
  • Language accessibility: Sinhala handwriting and multilingual spoken review need dedicated methods and evaluation.
  • Controlled improvement: feedback should reach durable memory or skills only after review and held-out validation.

Research problem

How can specialized AI learning components support personalized, accessible learning while keeping their outputs grounded and their updates verifiable?

LearnX studies this question through four independently developed subsystems, with biology as a principal learning domain. Component evaluation is distinguished from demonstrated improvement in learner outcomes.

Research objectives

Make each learning interaction more useful.

  1. Generate and validate interactive biology visuals, with region exploration and optional 3D reconstruction.
  2. Generate curriculum-grounded assessments, adapt difficulty and recommend topics for further practice.
  3. Support document-grounded spoken or typed revision in English, Sinhala and Tamil.
  4. Enhance unclear Sinhala handwriting, extract its text and generate multilingual study content.
  5. Evaluate each component and apply reviewed visualization updates only after held-out checks, with rollback available.

Methodology

Specialized pipelines. Evidence at each stage.

  1. 01Learning inputs

    Questions, study documents, voice and handwritten images.

  2. 02Specialized processing

    Generation, assessment, speech or handwriting pipelines.

  3. 03Grounding & validation

    Retrieved evidence, visual critique and component evaluation.

  4. 04Learning outputs

    Explorable visuals, practice questions, audio and recovered notes.

Current scope: the subsystems share architectural principles. A unified learner profile and single cross-component application remain future work. The validation-gated memory and skill update lifecycle is currently implemented for visualization.
